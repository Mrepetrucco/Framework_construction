1. **Cross-family agreement is not independent grounding.** If BX1 treats cross-family convergence as corroboration, it is grounding on a correlated chorus.

   - Conditional on both models being wrong, they give the **same wrong answer ~60%** of the time.
   - This dependence persists across architectures and providers, and **increases with model capability**. “Use larger/different-provider judges” therefore worsens rather than repairs the independence assumption.
   - Agreement is only weakly diagnostic: agreement–correctness \( \rho = .20\text{–}.59 \). A high-agreement outcome can still be a shared failure mode.
   - Exposure is class-dependent: corpus/data-gap failures are especially dangerous—**55.05% shared** in the Rust module class, Jaccard \(\sim .44\). Cross-family support on such claims is largely duplicate evidence.
   - No absolute false-grounding rate is identifiable without BX1’s base error rates and exact aggregation rule. But the relevant conditional exposure is already severe: among joint errors, approximately **3 in 5 apparent confirmations are identical wrong answers**, not independent checks.

   X2R’s deterministic DSL/hash controls may be sound as execution controls. They do not turn correlated model testimony into epistemically independent evidence.

2. **The fabricated-referent probe is primarily a prompt-sensitivity test, not a knowledge detector.**

   E2 directly undermines the interpretation that confabulation propensity is intrinsic model knowledge failure: citation fabrication was not spontaneous; it was **prompt-induced**. Thus BX1’s `.30` confabulation rate measures the interaction of fabricated referent, wording, task framing, and the subject model’s compliance behavior.

   The \( \geq 3 \)-model / 95.6% citation-consensus result does not rescue it:
   - it is a result for a specific prompted citation task and a specific consensus criterion;
   - it says repeated matching citations can be a useful *verification heuristic* in that task;
   - it does not establish that agreement detects nonexistent referents generally, nor that a single-model abstain/confabulate probe measures knowledge.

   BX1’s own evidence points to item/prompt effects: one referent produced **5/5** confabulations, while aggregate hedge rate was **0.00**. That is a non-calibrated forced-choice response pattern, not reliable epistemic self-monitoring.

3. **C6 can produce a selection-induced positive.** C2 routes only threshold-passing cases to an exemplar and sends below-threshold cases to a bare NULL stub. Therefore any shadow comparison of routed-X2R cases against NULL/stub cases conflates treatment with eligibility/difficulty:

   \[
   E[Y\mid \text{routed}] - E[Y\mid \text{NULL}]
   \neq \text{effect of X2R}.
   \]

   A positive can arise even if exemplars do nothing—or harm—because routed prompts are structurally more recognizable, more rule-matched, or otherwise easier. Shadow traffic also does not estimate user-facing causal impact because it returns no treatment prediction.

   **Cheapest repair:** within the same C2-eligible population, randomly assign **X2R exemplar vs bare-stub control**, pre-register one primary metric, and run an **A/A** first. For a selection/ranking outcome, use randomized interleaving instead. Do not compare routed and NULL populations as treatment arms.

4. **Most likely single-subject artefacts, ranked by exposure:**

   1. **Fabricated-referent result / hedge rate 0.00 — highest exposure.** One model, only \(6\times5=30\) observations, prompt-induced task, with a concentrated 5/5 item effect. The zero hedge rate is especially likely to be a model-and-prompt policy artifact rather than an architectural property.
   2. **T5 “stub carries the in-context effect.”** All reported levels are from gpt-4o-mini. The non-monotonic pattern—stub `.562`, partial `.625`, full `.531`—is consistent with one model’s context-format and instruction-following response, not evidence that the architecture’s minimal stub is generally causal.
   3. **Portability trap-raise, \(0/9,0/9,1/9,1/9\).** The effect is tiny, near floor, and each tier has only nine samples. It may be portable only in the weak sense that the test lacks resolution; it does not establish architectural robustness. Its single-subject exposure is lower only if the sweep genuinely spans independently evaluated model families—otherwise it belongs near rank 1.

5. **Directly contradicted belief:** that **cross-family convergence can graduate or ground a rule because distinct providers supply independent confirmation**. E1 says provider and architecture diversity do not restore independence; stronger models’ errors are more correlated. Route 2 is therefore not merely weak—it uses the wrong evidentiary model.
