# Adversarial Review #2: Attacking the Repaired Rule

**Scope conceded to the author:** matched-difficulty canaries, per-model stochasticity baselines, mechanism-diverse cross-model legs with measured correlation. I attack that version.

---

## (a) The matching regress: your matcher is an unvalidated oracle

"Matched difficulty" presupposes a difficulty *measure*. Difficulty is model-relative (inverse scaling, tokenization cliffs, RLHF-induced refusal regions), so:

1. **If you validate matching by model pass-rates**, you defined difficulty using the very models under test — the canary certifies the harness using an instrument calibrated on the thing the harness is measuring. Circular.
2. **If you validate by human judgment**, humans are demonstrably bad at predicting model-relative difficulty (they predict human-relative difficulty). You've replaced regress with fiat.
3. **Worse than the regress:** matching on difficulty/topic doesn't match on the *latent trigger*. Harness defects are frequently surface-correlated — prompt position, delimiter handling, context length, an escaping bug on a character class. A canary matched on difficulty and topic but not on the triggering surface feature passes while real items fail. Your discriminator table then reads "passes control → not a harness defect" and misattributes a harness defect to the model. **The matched canary produces confident false exoneration of the harness — strictly worse than no canary, which at least leaves you uncertain.**

The regress is real, but the fatal issue is (3): matching is matching *on the wrong axes* whenever the failure mode is one you didn't anticipate — which is precisely when you need the control.

## (b) The per-model baseline is self-attestation with extra steps

The baseline is estimated by running **the same model through the same harness with the same decoding stack**. Therefore:

- Any harness defect that *inflates* variance (nondeterministic truncation, race conditions in batching, retry-on-timeout silently resampling) inflates the baseline identically. The baseline **absorbs and normalizes the defect**. You will call pathologically noisy behavior "within stochastic baseline" because the pathology defined the baseline.
- Variance is not a global scalar. It is prompt-region-dependent (near-boundary prompts have structurally higher variance). A baseline estimated on baseline prompts does not transfer to the abstention region — which is *exactly* the high-variance region, by construction.
- API-served models are non-stationary. Your baseline has an unstated expiry date and no invalidation trigger.
- Most damning: a model with a **refusal attractor** (mode-collapsed, policy-driven abstention) produces *low* resample variance. Your table classifies stable = genuine. Stability discriminates **noise**, not **truth**. A model that stably, wrongly refuses passes every row of your table as "genuine abstention."

The baseline answers "is this model behaving like itself?" — self-attestation — when the question was "is this behavior correct?"

## (c) Costed abstention destroys the stability signal — provably

If abstention carries payoff cost in the selection loop, then for any item near the answer/abstain indifference point, the optimizer's optimum is a **mixed strategy**: abstain with probability p, guess with probability 1−p. Under n resamples this manifests as *high resample variance*. Your table classifies this as **silent non-adherence** — but it is the *rational, calibrated response of a well-behaved costed agent*. You will systematically punish calibration.

The reverse failure is worse: cost pressure converts abstentions into **hedged low-confidence answers**. The abstention doesn't become unstable — it *disappears*. You measure a confident-looking hallucination, your null-result machinery never fires, and the entire rule ("never accept a null without a control") is bypassed because there is no null.

Conclusion: **stability is only diagnostic when abstention is payoff-neutral, and the framework mandates it isn't.** The rule as written measures the cost schedule, not the model's epistemics.

## (d) The structural blind spot: **correlated confident error (consensus hallucination)**

The entire architecture is null-result-centric. Every discriminator row is about abstentions or identical control failures. There is **no row for a confident, wrong, cross-model-agreeing non-null answer**:

- low resample variance ✓ (it's a confident error)
- passes the known-answerable control ✓ (the control tests a different item)
- agrees cross-model ✓ (shared pretraining corpora, benchmark leakage, distillation lineage, shared common misconceptions)

Mechanism diversity with "measured correlation" doesn't save you: you measured correlation *on your test distribution*, and common-cause error is correlated precisely on the items your distribution undersamples. The architecture treats cross-model agreement as a proxy for ground truth; it is therefore **structurally blind to any falsehood the model population inherits in common** — which, given genealogical overlap of frontier models, is the dominant error class, not an edge case.

Secondary blindness, already implied above: **differential (canary-sparing) harness defects** and **temporal drift** (all checks are within-batch; nothing controls across time).

## (e) The architecture that survives this review

### Design principles
1. **Terminate the regress empirically, not by fiat.** The difficulty matcher becomes a *falsifiable predictive model*: it must predict per-model pass rates on held-out items within stated CIs. When prediction fails, the matcher is retrained and all verdicts issued under the stale matcher are flagged. Regress → finite loop scored out-of-sample.
2. **Validate the harness without the model.** Inject *synthetic responses* (canned model outputs with known correct parses/scores) directly into the pipeline. These harness-canaries need no difficulty matching because no model is involved — this kills both (a)'s regress and (a)(3)'s surface-mismatch problem for the parsing/scoring path. Run two independently implemented harnesses; harness bugs decorrelate, model behavior doesn't.
3. **Ground variance externally.** Decompose: temperature-0 fixed-seed runs measure the *infrastructure noise floor*; controlled-temperature ladders measure model noise against known injected stochasticity. Baseline invalidated automatically on any model version/hash change. Variance thresholds estimated *per prompt-region* (nearest-neighbor in embedding space), never globally.
4. **Payoff-isolate the measurement.** Two-regime protocol: **cost-off shadow evaluation** for diagnosis (stability is only interpreted here), **cost-on** for selection. The *delta* between regimes is itself a monitored signal — it measures policy pressure and catches abstention→hedge conversion. Confidence elicitation / token-entropy monitors catch hedged answers that bypass the null pathway.
5. **Validate non-nulls, not just nulls.** (i) Stratified audited ground truth on confident answers. (ii) **Consensus-trap canaries**: items whose documented common misconception is the attractor — a model population that agrees on the trap answer exposes its common-mode error rate. (iii) Verification by *consequence* where possible (execute the code, check the proof, run the query) — truth by mechanism, not by vote. (iv) Lineage-discounted agreement: cross-model agreement weighted down by training-genealogy overlap.

### Capturing failure modes it was not designed for
- **Residual quarantine bucket:** any (variance, control, agreement, entropy, latency) signature not matching a known table row is *never* force-classified. It is quarantined with a triage SLA. Every triaged case must terminate in either a new table row or a new canary class — the taxonomy is append-only and incident-driven.
- **Distribution-shift alarms:** monitor the *joint distribution* of all signals and alarm on drift regardless of classification. You don't need to name a failure mode to detect that the world changed.
- **Adversarial rotation:** a standing red-team role whose deliverable is items the current table misclassifies. Each success becomes a regression canary. The table's error rate against the red team is the architecture's health metric.

### Split: universal vs per-model

**Universal (valid for ALL models — owned by the harness team):**
- Synthetic-response harness canaries; deterministic replay; prompt checksums; dual-harness cross-replication
- Two-regime (cost-on/cost-off) protocol and regime-delta monitoring
- Consensus-trap item bank; audited-ground-truth sampling design; consequence-based verifiers
- Lineage-discount computation method; residual quarantine + triage process; drift alarms; red-team loop
- Temperature-0 infra noise-floor measurement procedure

**Per-model-owned parameter sets (recalibrated on every version change, auto-invalidated on hash change):**
- Difficulty-matcher calibration curve + its out-of-sample prediction score
- Prompt-region-conditional variance thresholds; n-resample budget; temperature ladder
- Refusal-attractor fingerprint (phrasing/embedding cluster distinguishing policy abstention from epistemic abstention)
- Lineage/overlap weights vs. every other model in the ensemble
- Cost-regime delta baseline (this model's measured policy-pressure term)

### Verdict on the original rule
Keep the instinct ("never accept a null without a control"), discard the table. Its three rows are not exhaustive, its "passes control" column exonerates too much, and its stability column reverses sign the moment abstention is costed. The surviving version validates the harness model-free, grounds variance externally, isolates payoff from measurement, audits non-nulls, and — critically — refuses to classify what it doesn't recognize.
