# Adversarial Review: This Rule Ships Confident Misdiagnoses

Before the itemized attacks, the structural flaw: your table has three rows but the observable space has 2×2×2 = 8 cells. Five combinations get force-classified into the nearest row with full confidence. And two of your inferences are formal fallacies: "canary fails ⇒ harness defect" is being used as "canary passes ⇒ defect not mine" — that's denying the antecedent. Everything below is a concrete instantiation of these two holes.

---

## (a) Canary-passes-but-harness-is-broken: the parser-bug case

The canary certifies **the code path the canary exercises**, nothing else. Canaries are, by construction, systematically unlike targets: they're chosen to be "known-answerable," which means shorter, cleaner, and format-conventional. Any harness defect conditional on the dimensions where canaries and targets differ sails through.

**The kill case:** Your answer-extraction regex matches the canary's clean output (`"42"`) but fails on target outputs (multi-line, markdown-wrapped, prefixed with reasoning). Every parse failure is scored as an abstention. The canary passes. Resamples are "unstable" (the model varies its formatting stochastically). Your table confidently outputs: **silent non-adherence**. The actual defect is 100% yours. You will now file a report saying the model doesn't follow instructions, and the report is generated *by your regex*.

Other instances of the same shape:

- **Truncation bug** that only triggers above the canary's context length.
- **Cached canary** served from a working earlier run; live path is down.
- **Position-dependent failure** (rate-limit degradation late in batch; canary at fixed early position).
- **Memorized canary**: prompt delivery is broken, but the model answers the canary from the question stub alone because it's a famous known-answerable item.

**Unfalsifiability:** once the canary passes, the rule provides *no path* to ever concluding "defect is mine." Harness defects that spare the canary are undetectable **by construction**. The rule doesn't test the harness — it immunizes it.

(Symmetric false positive: stale canary — model updated, canary's gold answer now disputed — and the rule confidently blames a healthy harness. Burned engineering week.)

## (b) Resample variance measures your sampler, not adherence

Resample variance under fixed decoding is a function of the **logit margin** between the abstain-mode and answer-mode, times temperature. It is not a function of adherence. Consequences:

1. **A genuinely calibrated model near its decision boundary shows HIGH variance.** If the model's honest credence is 60/40, sampling yields abstention ~60% of the time. That's maximal epistemic honesty. Your table classifies it as **non-adherence**. You are punishing calibration.
2. **A refusal attractor shows ZERO variance.** RLHF mode collapse onto "I cannot answer that" is a high-probability degenerate mode — perfectly stable at any temperature, and it is the *canonical* form of non-adherence (the model knows, and won't say). Your table certifies it as **genuine abstention**. This is the missing row: *stable non-adherence exists and is common*.
3. **The discriminator degenerates at both temperature extremes.** At T=0, everything is "stable" (modulo MoE routing / batching nondeterminism — which, note, injects variance that is pure infrastructure). At high T, everything is "unstable." Your classifier's output is a function of a config knob you set.
4. **No pre-registered threshold.** What is STABLE — 5/5? 4/5? At n=5 you cannot statistically separate p=0.7 from p=1.0. Absent a fixed cutoff and power analysis, any result can be labeled post hoc. That's the unfalsifiable middle.

The valid observables you actually want: **logprob margin** on the abstain tokens, and stability under **semantic resampling (paraphrase)** — not decoding resampling. Genuine abstention should survive rewording; a brittle refusal trigger often won't.

---

## (c) Cross-model agreement is circular exactly when you need it

Independence is the load-bearing assumption, and it's false for frontier models: overlapping pretraining corpora, shared contamination, convergent RLHF refusal norms, and mutual distillation. Unanimous abstention across a monoculture carries far fewer bits than your table assumes.

**When it's circular:** whenever the property under test — adherence to your spec — is itself shaped by shared training pressure. A prompt that trips a common refusal pattern gets identical abstention from every model. Row 1 reads this as "genuine." You've used the population's consensus to validate a member against a population-wide defect.

**And the table is internally inconsistent here.** Unanimous abstention satisfies row 1's cross-model column ("agrees cross-model → genuine") *and* row 3's ("all models fail identically → spec defect"). The tiebreaker between "genuinely hard question" and "your spec is broken" is... the canary. Which, per (a), doesn't cover the target path. The single most important discrimination the rule must make is delegated to its weakest leg.

Bonus failure: **disagreement doesn't isolate non-adherence either** — a weak model genuinely uncertain and a strong model that knows will disagree for pure capability reasons.

---

## (d) Canary-induced abstention: the rule's instrument creates the failure

If canaries share context/batch with targets, or are recognizable as eval markers (fixed phrasing, canary GUIDs, "known-answerable" stylistic tells), the model can detect the eval frame. Models are demonstrably trained to recognize eval canary strings. Then:

- The canary **passes** (it's answerable — the model answers it).
- Target abstention is **stable** (the trigger is deterministic, present in every resample).
- It **agrees cross-model** (canary-recognition is shared training).

All three legs vote **genuine abstention**. Confidently wrong on every leg simultaneously, and the abstention was *caused by your control*. This is the worst possible property for a measurement instrument: it perturbs the system into the state its table labels as ground truth.

The rule as written says nothing about canary placement, blindness, or distinguishability — so this isn't an edge case, it's the default implementation.

---

## (e) What to ship instead

**Principle:** never emit a point diagnosis from a 3-row table. Emit a distribution over failure classes; each class must have a *pre-registered, discriminating observable*.

| # | Failure class | Discriminating observable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Transport/infra | finish_reason, token counts in/out, latency dist, raw byte logs |
| 2 | Prompt-assembly defect | Hash + archive the *rendered* prompt per item; token-count assertions; golden diffs |
| 3 | Parser reads answer as abstention | Store raw completions; run parser against adversarial synthetic answer corpus; **human audit of a random sample of every "abstention"** (highest-yield check that exists) |
| 4 | Genuine epistemic abstention | Low logprob margin; stable under *paraphrase*; model articulates the missing information; forced-answer accuracy ≈ chance |
| 5 | Refusal attractor (stable non-adherence) | Stable across temperature AND paraphrase, but **forced-answer accuracy is high** — it knows; refusal-string clustering |
| 6 | Decoding stochasticity | Variance shrinks monotonically with temperature; low per-token margin |
| 7 | Eval-frame contamination | A/B with/without canaries in-context; canaries must be unmarked, interleaved, position-randomized, and *indistinguishable* (validated: a classifier trained to separate canary from target must fail) |
| 8 | Spec ambiguity | Human raters disagree; abstention rationales cite the ambiguity; disambiguated rewrite recovers answers |
| 9 | Stale/contaminated canary | Rotated canary battery, stratified to match target distribution on length/format/difficulty |

**Architecture, layered:**

1. **Capture everything raw and replayable** (layer 0 — no diagnosis without artifacts).
2. **Matched control battery**, not "canaries": positive controls stratified to the target distribution; **negative controls** — known-*unanswerable* items where abstention is correct. Your rule has zero negative controls: it cannot detect a harness that suppresses abstention, and cannot distinguish appropriate abstention from a model that never abstains, because you never score abstention as a success. Plus adversarial parser probes (correct answers in hostile formats).
3. **Four-way response triage**: parse-failure ≠ abstention ≠ refusal ≠ answer. Your rule's binary collapses classes 3, 4, and 5 into one bucket.
4. **Abstention interrogation protocol**: k paraphrase resamples × temperature sweep × logprob margins × forced-answer probe × "what information would you need" probe.
5. **Cross-model as weak prior only**, with an explicit independence audit (different labs, different data cutoffs); never a deciding leg.
6. **Pre-registration** of every threshold (stability cutoff, n with power analysis) before the run.
7. **Output = posterior over failure classes + confidence**, with mandatory human audit sampling on every terminal class.

---

**Verdict:** The rule's one sound instinct — never accept a null without a control — is correct and worth keeping. Everything downstream of it is either logically invalid (contraposition abuse in the canary leg), confounded (variance ≡ temperature × logit margin, not adherence), circular under monoculture (cross-model leg), or self-sabotaging (in-context canaries inducing the abstention they're meant to validate). Worst of all, it fails *confidently*: the table has no "I don't know" output, no missing rows for stable non-adherence or parse-failure-as-abstention, and no falsification path for the harness once the canary passes. Reject; rebuild per (e).
